Bathroom Water Damage: What Smyrna Homeowners Should Know

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. Once caulk splits, grout thins out or the toilet seal gives up, each shower and flush sends a bit of water through. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. In Smyrna, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

Because the leak is usually slow, the damage tends to be older and deeper than it looks. Subfloor under a toilet can be soft all the way through while the tile above still looks fine. With thermal imaging and moisture meters, we map what is wet before any demolition, and we open just the sections that will not dry in place.

Signs to Look For

  • Peeling paint or swollen baseboard on the wall next to the shower
  • Directly under the bathroom, a ceiling with a brown stain or a sag
  • Musty odor in the bathroom that cleaning does not get rid of
  • Dark grout that never seems to dry out between showers
  • Vinyl flooring that curls at the edges or has discolored near the toilet or tub
  • A warped or swollen cabinet floor under the vanity sink

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first few days

Water Slips Through

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. Backer board and the outer rim of the subfloor take it in, and there is still nothing to see.

First few weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

One to three months

Signs Show Up

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months and beyond

Structure Weakens

Subfloor around the toilet or tub turns soft and joists may begin to rot. Drying alone no longer fixes it, because carpentry and new tile are now part of the repair.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

A Failed Shower Pan

A cracked pan or liner lets water out under the shower floor every time it is used. Up top everything looks fine, yet the framing underneath never dries.

Damage Downstairs

The stain on the lower ceiling can be several feet off from the leak itself, because water travels along pipes and joists first. Its path can cross electrical wiring and ceiling lights.

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Behind the towels and bottles kept under the sink, a drain fitting or supply valve can drip for months. The cabinet floor swells, and the water runs through to the subfloor and the wall behind it.

Poor Ventilation

A weak or missing exhaust fan leaves steam in the room after every shower. That moisture settles on walls and ceilings, feeds mold, and slows the drying of anything that is already wet.

What We Do

How We Handle Bathroom Water Damage

1

Find the Leak

Each fixture is tested separately, from the supply lines to the toilet, tub and shower, and thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went beneath tile and in the walls.

2

Open What Is Needed

Soft wall board, loose tile and subfloor that has rotted are removed. If the toilet was the source, we handle that section as contaminated, and porous materials we cannot clean go.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the subfloor, the wall framing and the ceiling space below. Before anything gets closed up, antimicrobial is applied and dry readings are confirmed.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

We replace subfloor and backer board, and can coordinate tile, a new toilet seal, fresh caulk and ceiling repair so the leak path is closed, not covered over.
