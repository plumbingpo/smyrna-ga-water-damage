Wet Carpet Drying in Smyrna

A wet carpet is hiding a lot more water than you can see. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. For Smyrna homes, our crew handles the extraction, meters the carpet, pad and subfloor, and runs drying gear that gets to the bottom layer.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. If the water came out of a clean supply line and we reach it fast, the carpet can normally stay down while it dries. Water from an appliance counts as gray, so the pad is frequently removed while the carpet is cleaned and treated. After sewage or flood water from outdoors, neither the carpet nor the pad stays.

Signs to Look For

  • Dark, rusty lines along the wall where the tack strip has gotten wet
  • Days after the water was cleaned up, the carpet is cool and clammy to the touch
  • A squishy feel under your feet, or wet socks after walking across the room
  • Brown or yellow stains wicking up to the tips of the fibers as it dries
  • Carpet that has come off the tack strip, leaving loose areas or ripples
  • The damp area gets wider over time with no new spill to explain it

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Edges

We trace how far the water traveled with moisture meters and thermal imaging, including under walls and into closets. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Extract the Water

We use weighted extraction tools that squeeze water from carpet and pad at once. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry Every Layer

Some jobs call for floating the carpet on a cushion of air, others for lifting it and taking the pad out. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Mold Under the Carpet

The dark, damp underside of the carpet is where mold usually begins. You smell it long before you ever see it on top.

Wet Wall Bottoms

Water that runs under the carpet reaches the base of the walls. It wicks up into the baseboards and drywall unnoticed, since the carpet hides that edge.

Water Quality Drops

Even clean water goes bad the longer it sits. As it sits in carpet and pad it picks up soil and bacteria, so a carpet that could have been dried may have to be removed.

Swollen Subfloor

Left wet, the OSB or particleboard beneath a carpet swells and then crumbles. Squeaks, soft spots and raised seams show up weeks after the spill is forgotten.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Water Spreads Sideways

The pad wicks water well past the visible wet spot. The tack strip starts taking on water as it creeps below the walls toward the room next door.

24 to 48 hours

Smell and Mold Start

The damp pad breeds bacteria, and the sour smell of wet carpet follows. Mold can begin growing on the backing and on dust trapped deep in the fibers.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

Past one week

Past Saving

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. Often mold has spread to the subfloor and the base of the drywall, which adds to the job.
