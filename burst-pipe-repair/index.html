Pipes that burst rank among the most frequent causes of water damage, and they can do devastating harm to a building. At 4 to 8 gallons a minute, just one burst can send 240 to 480 gallons each hour into your ceilings, walls and flooring. Pipes burst from freezing, old plumbing, too much water pressure, corrosion or a physical hit, and in every case a fast emergency response is what keeps water from ruining flooring, structural materials, building systems and your belongings.

When a pipe bursts in Smyrna or the surrounding area, Smyrna Restoration runs an emergency response around the clock. We can help find the shutoff and stop the water, and our team works with licensed plumbers to see that the pipe itself is repaired correctly. With the source under control, our crew goes straight to extraction, using truck-mounted machines and portable extractors to clear standing water from each affected area quickly.

Following extraction, our structural drying process addresses all moisture-affected materials, including drywall, insulation, wood framing, subflooring and finished flooring. Burst pipes often damage multiple rooms and multiple levels, because water follows gravity through wall cavities, floor assemblies and ceiling spaces. Our thermal imaging and moisture detection equipment traces water migration paths so every affected area is identified and properly dried, which prevents the mold growth and structural deterioration that hidden moisture causes.

Maintenance Tips for Burst Pipe Repair

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Insulate exposed pipes in unheated areas such as attics, garages and crawl spaces
  • Replace old galvanized steel or polybutylene pipes before they fail unexpectedly
  • Check water pressure with a gauge, since anything over 80 PSI puts stress on fittings and pipes
  • Find the main shutoff valve now and practice closing it so you can do it fast
  • Outdoor faucets should be closed, drained and free of hoses ahead of freezing weather
  • When it freezes, let taps drip and open the cabinets under sinks to let warm air reach the pipes

Why Burst Pipe Repair Is Not a DIY Job

Burst pipe water damage often extends far beyond what is visible on the surface. It moves between ceiling joists, inside floor assemblies, beside electrical conduit and down wall cavities, soaking materials you might not find for days or even weeks. By then, mold may already be established in hidden spaces and structural materials may be significantly weakened. Professional restoration technicians use thermal imaging cameras and moisture meters to trace water migration and identify all affected areas immediately.

The machines we use for extraction and drying are in another class from anything sold for home use. Truck-mounted extractors remove water hundreds of times faster than a shop vacuum, and commercial dehumidifiers pull 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential units. Mold can begin growing in 24 to 48 hours, so this kind of power and speed matters a great deal.

Signs It Is Time for Burst Pipe Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Tap water that turns brown, yellow or cloudy
  • Visible water spraying or dripping from exposed pipes
  • Frost or ice on exposed pipes during cold weather
  • A hidden leak often shows up first as a much larger water bill
  • Water pooling on floors with no apparent source
  • No water at a tap in freezing weather, a sign the line is frozen
  • A sudden drop in water pressure throughout your property
