Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Know the location and condition of your sewer cleanout for emergency access
  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog
  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink
  • Do not wait on a slow drain, which can turn into a full blockage
  • Roots from trees close to the sewer line work their way into pipes and block them, so have them cleared

Why Hire a Professional for Sewage Cleanup?

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet
  • People in the household getting stomach or intestinal symptoms with no clear cause
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors
  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line
  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one
  • Sewage residue visible around drain openings or on basement floors
  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs
