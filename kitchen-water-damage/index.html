Kitchen Water Damage in Smyrna

Count the dishwasher, disposal, drain, faucet and fridge line, and the kitchen has more places for water to connect than anywhere else in the house. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. When one drips, the water runs to the back of the cabinet base and spreads under the floor. Our Smyrna crew locates the leak, dries what it wet, and keeps as much of your kitchen intact as we can.

In a wet kitchen, the cabinets are where the real expense lies. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. We pull toe kicks and drill small hidden holes to move dry air under and behind the cabinets, so in many kitchens the cabinets and countertops stay right where they are while everything dries.

Signs to Look For

  • Cabinet doors that stick or no longer line up with each other
  • Warping, staining or a soft spot in the floor of the cabinet below the sink
  • Laminate or wood floor that is lifting or cupping where you stand at the dishwasher
  • A water bill that climbs, or a faint hiss or drip sound, with every tap turned off
  • A damp, stale smell when you open the cabinet under the sink
  • Swelling and flaking where the cabinet sides meet the floor

How We Fix It

Our Kitchen Water Damage Process

1

Shut Off the Leak

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Map the Moisture

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry Without Demolition

With the toe kicks off, air gets pushed below the cabinets and behind them. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Remove Only Damage

We carefully remove any flooring, subfloor or cabinet section that cannot be saved. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

The Dishwasher Cavity

Slowly dripping supply or drain fittings soak the cabinet sides beside the unit and the subfloor below. None of it is visible behind the appliance until the flooring starts to change.

Layers of Flooring

Many kitchens have a newer floor installed on top of an older one. Trapped water sits between the two, with no way to evaporate and no sign on the surface.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

Behind the dishwasher and sink, the wall carries both the drain and the supply lines. Cabinets fully cover that drywall and framing, so a leak can soak them and mold can spread out of sight.

Refrigerator Water Line

An ice maker is fed by a thin tube that may lose a few drops each minute. When the fridge never gets moved, this amount will rot the flooring and the wall.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first hour

Water Runs Under

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. In the dark, enclosed gap below the cabinets, and at the back of the dishwasher, mold can start.

Day 3 to 7

Floors Begin Moving

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. Drawers and cabinet doors stop lining up.

After a week

Tear Out Likely

Swollen cabinet boxes cannot be brought back. Mold at the back of the cabinets often forces their removal, countertop included, and the subfloor may be delaminating.
